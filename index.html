MTechno Scrollbar

Scroll this page.Then look atthe scrollbar.

MTechno is a userscript that replaces the browser's scrollbar with a glass overlay, eases mouse-wheel scrolling, and marks headings, selected words and your own pins on the track. It is running on this page right now.It is switched off for now. Open the menu at the top right and switch it back on.

Free. One file, no network access. Works with Tampermonkey and Violentmonkey.

The scrollbar is on the right edge. Hover it.This is the browser's own scrollbar.

A scrollbar that stays out of the way

The bar sits flush against the right edge of the window. It fades out when you stop scrolling and comes back when you scroll or move the pointer to the edge. Drag the thumb and a label shows how far into the page you are and which section you are in.

Click the empty track and the page moves one screen toward the pointer. Hold the button and it keeps going until the thumb reaches you, the way a Windows scrollbar does.

The thumb follows the light. This band is pale, so the thumb is dark. The next one is deep blue, so it turns to clear glass.

  • Hover the edge

    The thumb widens, the track shades above it to show your progress, and a tick appears for every heading.

  • Drag the thumb

    One to one with the page. The label shows the section and the percentage.

  • Click the track

    One page toward the pointer. Hold to repeat.

  • Look right

    When a page is wider than the window, a matching bar appears along the bottom.

Wheel scrolling that eases

Each wheel notch moves a target and the page glides to it, at the same pace on any display. Spin the wheel quickly and it picks up speed, up to two and a half times one notch. Reverse the wheel and it turns around at once instead of finishing the old move. A trackpad is passed through one to one.

Pick a preset under the trace, then scroll. The trace shows what the page really did.

A map of the page, on the track

Headings appear as small ticks on the scrollbar while the pointer is over it. Hover a tick to read the heading, click it to go there. Every section of this page is on the bar.

If a page has a fixed header, jumps stop below it instead of hiding the heading underneath.

Move by section

These buttons send Alt Up and Alt Down. On a real page you press the keys yourself.

Select a word, see where else it appears

Select a word and every other place it appears is marked on the track in amber, the colour browsers use for find results. Hover a mark to read the sentence around it. Click it to go there.

Whole words only: selecting "art" does not mark "party". Marks show up when a word occurs at least twice.

Think of the track as a map. The thumb shows where you are on the track. A heading tick shows where a section starts on the track. An amber mark shows where your word appears on the track.

The word "track" appears on this page.

Pins for places you want to come back to

Press Alt M to drop a pin where you are. Pins are blue flags to the left of the track. Move between them with Alt Shift Down and Up. Press Alt M again near a pin to remove it.

Each pin remembers the section it sits in, and pins are kept for each page.

Pick up where you left off

On long pages the script remembers how far you read. Open the same page from the top later and a chip offers to take you back, with the saved position as a percentage.

It stays quiet on short pages, on links with a #hash, and when the browser has already restored your position.

And a way back

After a long jump (a click on a marker, the End key, a jump to a pin) a chip offers to return to where you were. It stays for seven seconds and disappears if you get back on your own.

The chip appears in the bottom right corner.

Panels that scroll on their own

Many web apps scroll inside a panel instead of the window. With the experimental switch on, the wheel eases inside those panels too. It is off by default because apps handle scrolling in many different ways.

Try the inbox with the switch off, then on.

Keyboard

  • UpDown

    Scroll 80 pixels.

  • Page UpPage DownSpace

    Scroll 88% of the window.

  • HomeEnd

    Top and bottom of the page.

  • AltUpDown

    Previous and next section.

  • AltM

    Add or remove a pin at the current position.

  • AltShiftUpDown

    Previous and next pin.

Keys are left alone while you type in a field or use a button, and whenever Ctrl or Meta is held.

It steps aside when it should

  • You hold Ctrl, Shift, Alt or Meta

    The browser handles the wheel, so zoom and horizontal scrolling work as usual.

  • The wheel is over a scrollable panel

    The panel scrolls first. When it reaches its end, the page takes over after a short pause.

  • Focus is in a text field or on a button

    Keys keep their normal job.

  • A page ships its own smooth scrolling

    Lenis, Locomotive Scroll, fullPage.js and similar engines are detected and left alone.

  • A page ignores scroll commands

    After three attempts where nothing moves, the browser's own scrolling is used for a minute.

  • The script fails five times

    It removes its scrollbar and restores the native one for that page.

  • The machine is slow

    If frames take too long, Lite mode removes the background blur.

  • Windows high contrast is on

    The bar, markers and labels switch to system colours.

What it keeps, and where

Nothing leaves your computer. The script makes no network requests, and this page loads nothing from other sites either.

Your settings, reading positions and pins live in the userscript manager's own storage. Reading positions and pins include the page address, without the #hash and common tracking parameters. Private windows are not detected, so positions are saved there too.

Install it

  1. Get a userscript manager

    Tampermonkey is the one the script is written for. Violentmonkey should work too.

  2. Open the script file

    On GitHub, open advanced-scrollbar.user.js and choose Raw. Your manager offers to install it.

  3. Press Install, then reload a page

    The scrollbar appears on every http and https page. Use the manager's menu to change settings.

Questions

Does it work on every site?

On every http and https page where your manager can run scripts, in the top-level window. It does not run inside embedded frames, and managers cannot run on browser-internal pages, extension stores or built-in PDF viewers.

Will it slow pages down?

It does its work while you scroll, and the page scans it performs are split into short slices. On slow machines Lite mode removes the costly background blur.

Can I turn it off on one site?

Yes. The manager menu has a command to disable it for the current site. Use it in this page's menu to compare with normal scrolling.

Does it work on a touch screen?

It is built for a mouse and keyboard. Touch scrolling is left to the browser.

Where do the settings live?

In the manager menu. Speed, markers, the resume chip, visual effects and the experimental panel smoothing can all be changed there.

This page runs the real userscript through a small stand-in for the manager's menu and storage. The only change is that the script is allowed to run inside a frame.
